The Theory of Electrodynamics in General Relativity

Conductor: Mobin Naderi


The presentation of this circle will unfold in two acts. We begin by outlining the path of the sessions.

Act I: We will first develop tensor calculus alongside special relativity, and then build up to general relativity and its mathematics at the level of tensor analysis. Electromagnetism will be reformulated in covariant form and expressed in the language of both special and general relativity. Finally, we will derive Einstein’s field equations and their electro-vacuum solution.

Act II: The mathematics employed in the first act requires further refinement. We will therefore revisit the notions of tensors, metrics, and related concepts. Beginning with linear algebra, we will introduce the basics of topological spaces and then move on to manifolds, since Einstein’s equivalence principle naturally calls for a formulation in the language of manifolds. With differential forms in hand, we will return to Maxwell’s equations and construct their geometric form. Ultimately, we will explore derivatives in relativity more carefully, which will allow us to discuss spacetime symmetries. Building on this, we will linearize Einstein’s equations and, using spacetime symmetries, introduce gravitational waves.

In summary, our goal is to restate everything presented in Act I in tensorial language, but this time fully within the framework of differential geometry.


Reference:The sources for this circle are broad and varied, but the main references will be shared in the study group.


Prerequisites:We will try to cover the essential prerequisites throughout the sessions themselves. However, prior familiarity with classical electromagnetism and linear algebra is required.
